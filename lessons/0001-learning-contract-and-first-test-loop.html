Lesson 0001: Learning Contract And First Test Loop

Your first job is not to build an agent. It is to prove that you can use the curriculum like an engineer: inspect the contract, run the feedback loop, and explain the next smallest move.

Mission Tie

You are learning AI Engineering for job readiness, Business AQL and FinAgent-style product building, and stronger agentic systems judgment. That means every lesson needs evidence: code, tests, traces, notes, or a defensible explanation.

Today you will anchor yourself in the main track and verify that the test loop works before touching implementation.

Core Idea

Job-ready AI Engineering is not "make the model do something impressive." The core loop is:

Step Question Evidence
Inspect What is the smallest contract? README, tests, rubric, scope guard
Predict What should fail first? A written guess before running tests
Run What does the feedback loop say? Focused pytest output
Change What is the smallest useful edit? One function, one test, one trace, or one note
Explain What did this prove and not prove? Short reflection or PR-style summary

Practice

From the repository root, run:

python -m pytest --collect-only curriculum/main-track/00-python-foundations -q

Then open:

curriculum/main-track/00-python-foundations/week-00-diagnostic/README.md

Before running any diagnostic tests, write three lines in your own notes:

  1. What capability is this diagnostic checking?
  2. What kind of failure would mean "I need Python repair" rather than "the curriculum is broken"?
  3. How does this connect to FinAgent or Business AQL product work?

Retrieval Check

Without looking back, answer these:

  1. What is the difference between the main track and extended concepts?
  2. Why does the course start with deterministic behavior before model calls?
  3. What evidence would convince a reviewer that you learned something today?

Stop Rule

Do not ask AI to complete the diagnostic for you. Ask AI to explain a failing test, define a term, or help you choose the next smallest change. You are training engineering judgment, not outsourcing it.
