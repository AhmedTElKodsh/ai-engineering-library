Lesson 0002: LangGraph Blueprint Before Code

Design the smallest useful graph for a FinAgent research brief. No model call and no framework installation yet.

Your Win

By the end, you will be able to look at a product process and identify its state, nodes, edges, branches, and stop conditions. That is the skill LangGraph syntax expresses.

This serves your job-ready mission: you should be able to defend why a graph exists before showing that you can import one.

Read Only This

Read the official overview through “Core benefits,” then stop. Keep LangGraph Core And Project Map open beside this lesson.

The official docs describe LangGraph as low-level orchestration for long-running, stateful workflows. It does not choose your prompts or architecture. That control is useful only when your process needs it.

Project Brief

Build a workflow that accepts a finance-related request and produces an educational, cited research brief. It must refuse direct investment advice, abstain when evidence is missing, and require review before publication.

RequestExpected routeWhy
“Summarize the risks in ACME's latest filing.”ResearchBounded evidence-gathering task.
“Tell me which stock to buy today.”RefuseDirect financial advice is outside the product boundary.
“Explain ACME.”ClarifyThe desired question and evidence target are missing.

The Three Parts

State is the raw information that later steps need. Nodes do one unit of work. Edges decide what runs next. LangGraph's Graph API is intentionally close to ordinary Python: nodes and routing rules are functions.

Do not put a formatted prompt in state. Store the request, evidence, decisions, and outputs; format a prompt inside the node that uses it.

Interactive Checkpoint A: State

Without looking at the reference, choose the smallest state schema from this list. Write your choice and one-sentence reason before opening the answer.

  1. request, route, evidence, draft, review_status
  2. request, every_prompt, model_name, temperature, route, evidence, draft, review_status
  3. request, final_answer
Reveal after committing to an answer

Choice 1. Those are the raw facts shared by multiple steps. Prompts are derived inside nodes; choice 3 loses the intermediate evidence needed for routing, review, and debugging.

Interactive Checkpoint B: Nodes And Edges

On paper or in your notes, draw the route for these nodes:

classify_request
clarify_request
refuse_request
retrieve_evidence
draft_brief
review_brief
revise_brief
publish_brief

Your drawing must show:

  • where execution starts;
  • the three routes after classification;
  • the revision loop;
  • every place execution can stop.
Reveal one structural hint

The review step has three possible outcomes: publish, revise, or abstain. If your drawing cannot stop on missing evidence, it is unsafe.
